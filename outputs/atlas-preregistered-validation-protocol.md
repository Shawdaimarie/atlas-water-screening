# ATLAS validation protocol for the 16 candidate study areas

**Protocol state: proposed; not externally preregistered.** Freeze a dated copy in a public or institutionally controlled registry before examining candidate-area OPERA outcomes or reference labels. Any change after that point must be versioned with a dated rationale. No area currently satisfies the confirmatory enrollment gates; this protocol defines the evidence needed to change that status.

## Objectives and inference limits

The study will assess (1) availability of QA-valid satellite observations, (2) classification of surface water, (3) event-period inundation relative to a matched non-event baseline, and (4) overlap of mapped water with licensed, dated infrastructure and service data. These are separate estimands. A product water class is not by itself flood truth; overlap is not damage, service interruption, or monetary loss. Value integration will preserve separate measures for hazard, exposure, service criticality, population served, distributional coverage, and decision usefulness. No composite social-value score will be calculated without locally approved weights and sensitivity analysis.

The 16 sites are purposively selected, eight in the United States and eight internationally. They are not a probability sample of cities, so results support comparisons among enrolled sites only and do not estimate national or global city prevalence. Sites may enter the confirmatory analysis only when they meet the same data, reference, sampling, and precision gates. If fewer than 8+8 qualify, report the enrolled subset as exploratory and do not describe the cross-region comparison as complete.

## Fixed measurement contract

- Use OPERA DSWx-S1 V1 at its documented 30 m grid and the binary water fraction raster (BWTR) for primary surface-water mapping. Interpret BWTR `0` and `1` as valid non-water and water classifications only where the product specification marks the observation valid. HAND-masked `250`, layover/shadow `251`, fill, and other no-data are missing observations, never dry land. Report product version, granule identifiers, acquisition time, processing metadata, grid alignment, mask classes, and valid-pixel fraction for every area/date.
- Use each candidate's fixed 25 × 25 km screening footprint (625 km² nominal), with a frozen equal-area projection/grid and the exact boundary file hash in the analysis manifest. Do not change boundaries after seeing mapped water. Report boundary clipping and pixel counts.
- Distinguish mapped surface water, persistent water, and event-associated inundation. An event inundation label must be supported by independent temporal/reference evidence; subtracting a single baseline classification alone does not establish floodwater.
- Report observation availability (valid area fraction and dates), water-map agreement, event-versus-baseline change, and asset/service overlap as separate results. Missing or ambiguous cells remain missing and are included in coverage accounting.

NASA's OPERA catalog/specification documents the BWTR classes and invalid masks, as well as the product-level validation requirement. That product requirement is not an ATLAS sample-size or AOI coverage rule. See the [OPERA DSWx-S1 Earth Engine catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/OPERA_DSWX_L3_V1_S1) and [OPERA validation plan](https://d2pn8kiwq2w21t.cloudfront.net/documents/OPERA_Validation_Plan.pdf).

## Candidate discovery and event enrollment

The prior 90-date metadata threshold is discovery bookkeeping only. It is not a site-enrollment, observation-adequacy, or statistical-power criterion. The three-date and 80%-valid-per-scene floors are feasibility checks only; show results below those floors as unavailable, and never describe passing them as statistically adequate.

Before opening candidate-area OPERA water outputs, identify qualifying flood events from independent, dated official event products, gauge/agency records, or independently mapped references. Archive the query, inclusion list, exclusions, event start/end, local timezone, UTC conversion, spatial intersection with the fixed AOI, reference provenance, license, and product availability. Use the first qualifying event under the frozen event catalog and common study window, or a reproducible random selection from the frozen eligible-event list. Do not select events based on OPERA response magnitude or apparent agreement.

The primary event reference acquisition must be within 24 hours of a documented event time. A 24–72-hour lag is sensitivity-only and must report the exact lag and hydrologic context; greater than 72 hours is excluded from the primary contemporaneous validation. These are ATLAS design choices, not universal remote-sensing standards. If timing cannot be established, the event is not primary-eligible.

For each event, select a non-event baseline before inspecting results, outside the event period and in a comparable seasonal/tidal/rainfall context. Record the rationale and meteorological/hydrologic context. Since exact acquisition dates are not common to all AOIs, use event-relative windows and the same number of matched QA-valid observations, selection rule, and window length across every enrolled area. Report the full scene list and any missingness. Three dates per period is only a feasibility floor; repeated acquisitions are not independent validation samples.

Confirmatory enrollment requires all of the following for every site in the comparison: (a) event and baseline pixels pass documented product QA; (b) primary event timing is supported by an independent reference; (c) an independent reference source covers the fixed AOI and is licensed/usable; (d) spatially representative probability sampling can be drawn and adjudicated; and (e) the planned effective sample size/precision target can be met, or the study is explicitly labeled exploratory with achieved precision reported. Run the 90% valid-pixel threshold as a sensitivity analysis; retain valid fraction as a continuous metric in all cases.

## Reference labels and sampling

Reference units will be drawn from a probability sample stratified by the mapped water/non-water classes and, when available, urban/asset context. Oversample rare water and class boundaries as needed, retain each unit's inclusion probability, and use design weights in estimates. Determine spatial block size from a documented pilot variogram/autocorrelation analysis; adjacent pixels within a block are not independent units. Do not use a convenience set of positive pixels as an accuracy sample.

The reproducible frame builder is `scripts/build_reference_sample_frames.py`. Given the complete 16-area study package, each area's fixed AOI, and event and baseline one-band 30 m BWTR sampling rasters, it verifies valid pixels lie inside the fixed AOI and draws a stratified simple random sample without replacement separately for the two periods and both map classes. It writes pixel-centre coordinates, records each class population, sample size, inclusion probability, design weight, AOI and raster hashes, and seed. The package template lists the required fields. This implements the initial frame draw only: it does not supply or validate independent reference labels, establish spatial independence, or satisfy confirmatory enrollment. The default 385 is the unadjusted per-area-period planning count; revise the draw upward using pilot-estimated design effects before confirmatory use. When a class contains fewer pixels than its allocation, all available units are selected and the finite population is recorded.

Labels are `event_floodwater`, `persistent_water`, `nonwater`, `ambiguous`, or `unobservable`. Two trained labelers independently interpret each sampled unit while blinded to the ATLAS result; a third reviewer adjudicates disagreements. Preserve both initial labels, final label, reason, reference imagery/sensor/date/resolution, georegistration and timing uncertainty, interpreter identity/code, license, and traceable source URL. Do not force ambiguous or unobservable units into water/non-water classes.

Reference evidence must be independent of the tested water classification. Sentinel-1 hand labels that share the tested SAR acquisition/lineage are not independent truth unless the label-generation process provides demonstrably independent evidence. C2S labels that reuse the same Sentinel-1 scene can support a lineage-qualified sensitivity comparison, not independent primary accuracy. PlanetScope imagery may identify visible water but does not alone distinguish floodwater from persistent water; require a temporally matched flood-event reference. Report all license and lineage conflicts before enrollment.

## Precision, metrics, and uncertainty

For a simple random sample estimating a proportion, the worst-case planning calculation at 95% confidence and ±5 percentage-point half-width is `n0 = 1.96² × 0.25 / 0.05² = 384.16`, rounded up to **385 effective independent reference units per area and per period**. It is not a pooled 385-unit target across the study. For 16 areas and two separately evaluated periods (event and baseline), the starting design therefore requires 12,320 area-period reference assessments before design-effect, nonresponse, or finite-population adjustments. The same spatial locations may be sampled in both periods for a paired design, but each period needs its own valid reference labels and estimate. The 385-unit target covers only an overall proportion/accuracy estimate; it does not guarantee precision for rare water classes, user/producer accuracy, or other confusion-matrix metrics. Determine class-specific allocation from a pilot and desired precision before examining test labels. Inflate effective sample size for spatial clustering/design effects and apply a finite-population correction where applicable. If a target cannot be reached, provide achieved design-based confidence intervals and label the finding exploratory; do not report a target as achieved.

Report the raw confusion matrix and area-weighted error matrix, user's and producer's accuracies for water and non-water, overall accuracy, F1, intersection-over-union, and Matthews correlation coefficient, with design-consistent 95% confidence intervals. Report water class separately from event-floodwater class. Quantify label disagreement and reference uncertainty. Use spatial-block bootstrap or a design-consistent estimator that accounts for stratification, weights, and clustering; do not bootstrap individual adjacent pixels as if independent. For temporal/event contrasts, use matched observations and uncertainty clustered by event/site; disclose that the purposive site set limits external generalization.

This design follows good-practice guidance on probability sampling, reference response design, area-weighted accuracy estimation, and uncertainty, and recent flood-map validation research on spatial design and event-dependent metrics: [Olofsson et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2014.02.015); [Landwehr et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114439).

## Value integration and release of results

For each area, document data ownership/license, date and geometry completeness, spatial resolution, population/service coverage limits, local governance requirements, and agency/community review status. Summarize water-to-asset overlap with denominators and uncertainty. Add service criticality, population served, and vulnerability only with attributable, licensed sources and appropriate privacy safeguards. Compare findings with documented local priorities; do not infer realized benefit, reduced loss, customer demand, or causal service outcomes from spatial overlap.

Publish the protocol version/hash, frozen AOIs, eligibility decisions, event-selection log, full acquisition and exclusion manifest, QA masks/coverage, reference-label provenance/license, sampling frame and inclusion weights, adjudications, analysis code/environment, deviations, and area-level results. Independent reproduction should run from these artifacts. A score increase requires the canonical rubric's corresponding empirical evidence; a completed protocol or synthetic walkthrough earns no real-observation validation credit.
