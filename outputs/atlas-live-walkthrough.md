## Guided walkthrough

1. Open the Site and read the visible synthetic-data disclosure. Candidate-area profiles and the synthetic analysis scenario are separate controls; selecting a study candidate does not change the synthetic portfolio.
2. In **16-area evidence and coverage screen**, select **Jakarta**, **Dubai**, **Singapore**, and **Mumbai** in turn, then compare **Houston**. Review each fixed footprint, empirical public-need evidence, value-integration question and dimensions, comparable validation path, source links, and metadata count. Every profile remains candidate-only until its QA and independent-label gates pass.
   - For Singapore, the current PUB sources link a 36-location flood-prone list (Nov 2025) and a 19-location hotspot list (Feb 2026) as national research/management context. These named-place lists have no event polygons, and no overlap with the fixed ATLAS box is claimed. Open the profile’s **Open current PUB hotspot list** link or the full [machine-readable 8+8 evidence register](atlas-study-area-evidence-register.json) for all 16 profiles and their scope notes.
3. Review **Cross-sensor catalog availability · OPERA DSWx-HLS** near the bottom of the page. The table shows 16/16 public metadata queries, 10,978 scene records, and 228–425 dates per area for the same audit window. Download the compact CSV/JSON or open the full report. Treat this as discovery metadata only: the area-date counts are not valid-pixel measurements, detected water, events, or independent truth; authenticated raster access and AOI QA remain pending.
4. Read the **Shared public-value framework** beneath the selection matrix. It applies the same sequence to every area: hazard observation, asset exposure, service consequence, distributional equity, and decision usefulness. It keeps observations separate from consequences and states that water overlap alone does not show damage, failure, loss, or benefit.
5. Expand **Compare 12 accepted sampled dates for all 16 areas**. The table shows three Sentinel-1 RTC VV orbit groups per quarter in each fixed area. The coverage-conditioned matched quota reports **192/192 selected groups**, **16/16 areas**, and **34 additional candidates screened** (23 accepted and 11 below the 80% floor). The card separately reports 169/192 passing groups in the initial random draw and warns that matched selection is conditional, not an archive-wide pass-rate estimate. This is spatial availability only.
6. Expand **Raster-level candidate-date availability · Copernicus GFM**. The original 16-area table summarizes 48 baseline/event/post-event date slots. Then, under the reference-label coverage cards, open **Copernicus GFM · 16-area temporal coverage**. Its expanded audit summarizes 114 slots across all areas: 92 readable rasters, 17 meeting the 80% valid-pixel floor, and Houston alone passing the three-date pre/post availability screen. Houston and Jakarta each have a qualifying candidate-event-date raster. These dates come from unverified discovery leads, and GFM shares Sentinel-1 lineage with OPERA, so it is not independent flood truth.

7. In **Shared validation protocol**, review **Data governance and public value** and open its linked governance standard. The common controls cover FAIR provenance, CARE and locally defined Indigenous data-governance requirements where applicable, privacy/minimization, equity and missingness, and separation of hazard, exposure, service consequence, and decision value. The review state is pending for all 16 candidate areas; this is not community or agency endorsement.
8. Review the reference-label cards and shared protocol. The proposed design requires independently sourced event evidence, blinded labels with adjudication, probability sampling, and a predeclared precision target. It plans 385 effective independent reference units per site-period for an overall accuracy/proportion estimate, before design-effect and finite-population adjustments.
   - Open **Tampa · native OPERA BWTR pixels**. The card reports 497,316 AOI pixels in each native scene and links a provenance JSON and methods note. Compare 2024-09-14 (22.5026% classified water) with 2024-09-26 (22.7881%). Treat this as descriptive only: the two preliminary scenes do not meet the three-date-per-period floor and have no independent reference labels.
9. In **Study setup**, select **Baton Rouge / Hurricane Ida**. Confirm the 2021 event and baseline windows, Louisiana synthetic portfolio, and the synthetic-data disclosure.
10. Switch to **Quality-aware aggregate**. The lead asset changes from 28.6% to 21.4% illustrative overlap. Select **Southeast Water Plant** to see 18.9% overlap, 93% fixture coverage, and 73% uncalibrated fixture confidence. These are demonstration values, not measurements.
11. Select **Export evidence** and inspect the download; then select **Reset analysis**. The exported CSV should retain the scenario, observation windows, method, and synthetic-fixture label. Reset restores the single-observation method and default asset while preserving the chosen scenario.
12. Scroll to **Other public reference screens**. Inspect the live CEMS AOI screen, reference-data coverage cards, and linked source audits. These are discovery or source-availability screens; none of the listed leads is independent validation for the 16 candidate areas.
13. Review **ASEAN modeled flood-hazard context · 27 raster screens** and open the 27-row JSON or HTML methods report. These are 30 m design-event model outputs for Singapore, Jakarta, and Bangkok. Read the coverage and nodata caveats; the screen is exposure context, not an observed event or validation label.
14. Select **Sacramento**. Its profile links to the City of Sacramento's May 2024 flood-management plan and hypothetical levee-break maps. The 2017 event is outside the common observation window, the maps are modeled scenarios, and neither provides reference pixels; the profile remains candidate-only.
